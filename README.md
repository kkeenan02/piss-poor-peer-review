# A peer review, in full

The complete correspondence arising from a declined submission to a law journal, September 2026. Every document is reproduced verbatim and in sequence, so that any characterisation of the exchange can be checked against the thing itself. The submission under review is a legal commentary on relationships and sexuality education in Northern Ireland after *JR87* [2025] UKSC 40.

Reviewers and the journal's editor are unnamed. Nothing has been added, cut or reordered within any document; salutations and signatures are removed.

## The text analysis

Every evaluative statement in the documents that assess the manuscript is given one primary code. A statement is the unit, not a sentence: a sentence can carry more than one, and a clause can stand alone. The author's own letters (the appeal and the requests that the criticisms be named) carry no code, because the five codes describe how a submission is assessed, and applying them to the letters asking for that assessment would measure nothing.

The five codes:

- **Located**: names a place in the paper and says what is wrong with it. This is the only kind of criticism a revision can act on.
- **False**: asserts something the manuscript refutes on inspection.
- **Unlocated**: a negative claim about the manuscript with nothing to go and look at: a real defect is alleged but no locus is given, so a page reference would repair it.
- **Moralising**: judges propriety, character or standing rather than naming a repairable fault. Nothing could repair it, because the predicate is worth.
- **Pastoral**: addresses the author's state of mind rather than the submission.

Statements that are verdicts, procedural, or neutral restatements are left uncoded.

The boundary that does the most work is **unlocated** against **moralising**. The test is whether a defect is asserted at all. Unlocated means a real property of the manuscript is alleged and could in principle be pointed to. Moralising means no property is alleged, only rank, so nothing could repair it. A standard that is never stated is a discretionary judgement wearing the costume of a constitutive one, so "falls someway short of the standard required for publication" and "does not commend itself to any academic forum" are moralising, not criticism. The test is functional, not lexical: a condemnation phrased in the grammar of measurement is still moralising if no measurable defect is named.

On the page, a coloured rule beneath a statement marks its code. Hover over one, or tap it, for the code and the reason it was assigned. Every label carries its own justification, so a reader can disagree with any of them individually, and the counts change accordingly. The chart at the top shows the whole correspondence in sequence, one bar per document, composed by code.
